# X05 partial-run replacement paired evaluation

This five-pair Release public matrix compares baseline `bbf33c4d7d4982d3ab021937985f5982d9db6694` with candidate `a3b638198d71c581e9e19d278b787ac04be6ac6a`, using the unchanged benchmark trace v1 (`75deec6b0dbc221d03031c1c5d816e90d3eb1ef045a6a6f8f723b0e02c4c28ea`). All 54 public workload state and operation fingerprints matched. The matrix was public-only and did not rerun native benchmarks or recovery. Full environment, per-call samples, aggregate, and build/run logs are in [`raw/`](raw/).

For public 10-KB replacements after 3,000 prior replacements, the median paired ratios were 0.336 for mean latency, 0.018 for p50, and 0.930 for p99. That is roughly 3x lower mean and 55x lower p50 for this parent-to-candidate slice. The median per-run p99 moved from 20.38 ms to 18.46 ms, still far above X05's 2 ms ceiling. Candidate p99 was between 0.82x and 1.03x baseline across pairs, so the tail improvement is small and inconsistent. The exact-run parent had 145 fallback snapshots in the 200-call workload; the partial-run candidate's diagnostic build had 34. The remaining 17% fallback share explains why the mean and median improve much more than p99.

The no-history public 10-KB replacement mean ratio was 0.290, and its p50 ratio was 0.326, so the slice improved this light-history control. The byte-control no-history mean ratio was 0.312. These results show that splitting one targeted retained run is effective and does not regress the measured light-history means. They do not satisfy X05's full aged replacement target of 5x lower mean, the 2 ms p99 ceiling, or the mixed 5-KB/50%-deletion target.
